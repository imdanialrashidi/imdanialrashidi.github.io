Elsa Hamrah is a Persian-language online store for phones, tablets, smartwatches, headphones and accessories, run from Tehran. The client needed a complete public website, so I designed it and implemented it: homepage, category structure, product pages, cart and the supporting policy pages. The work shipped in 2024, and the store is live at elsahamrah.com.

The catalogue is wide and many models look alike to someone shopping casually. My aim was to make choosing feel less like guesswork. Categories give a first direction, brand filters narrow it, and a contact option sits beside products where the price needs a conversation.

## The brief

The brief had two halves. The first was navigation: a shopper arrives knowing roughly what they want, a phone, a watch, headphones or an accessory for one of them, and should reach a product without opening five menus. The second was confidence. Buying electronics online raises specific doubts about authenticity, warranty, returns and delivery, and the site had to answer them where people hesitate rather than in a distant help section. Put plainly, a first-time visitor should reach the cart with more confidence.

## What I built

The homepage leads with a smartwatch banner for Apple and Samsung models marked at 20 percent off, next to a live-offer card for a Xiaomi X7 phone that invites a call instead of showing a price. A short category strip below the banner leads into smartwatches, headphones and mobile phones. Under that runs a row of five trust signals: fast delivery, authentic goods, refunds, round-the-clock support and a price guarantee.

I organised the catalogue into mobile, tablet, watch, headphones and accessories. Shoppers can filter each category by brand, the menu surfaces Apple, Samsung and Xiaomi, and the header carries search, login and the cart. The utility bar and menu cover the practical routes: order tracking, refunds and returns, terms, frequently asked questions, about and contact, plus a blog. Contact appears in several places, including a phone number and an email address in the utility bar.

## Limits and what I did not measure

My work was design and implementation, not ongoing store operations. I added no analytics or tracking, so I have no traffic, conversion, cart abandonment or revenue data for this store, and I do not report any. I also did not audit stock accuracy, price updates, delivery performance or support response times, all of which depend on the client's team. Prices and availability move, and some products show no price at all until a customer contacts the store, so check the live site before relying on a specific item. This page describes the site I built and the structure I can verify from it.
